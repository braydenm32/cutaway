## New in 0.9.13: make your cameras match, tell identical cameras apart, and finer control in Edit layout

**Check cameras: make every camera match before the show**
- **Sources › Cameras › Check cameras…** looks at each camera in turn and tells you, in plain words, what to change on each one so they all match your main camera. Step out of the shot for two seconds, then hold a sheet of plain white paper where a guest sits; Cutaway does the rest.
- **It checks six things:** colour (warmer, cooler, greener or pinker than the main camera), brightness, whether the picture is too dark or bright, flicker from the room lights, grey or crushed blacks, and focus and grain. Each camera gets a card with a green, amber or red mark and its most important fixes, and a strip along the top shows every camera's white page side by side.
- **It only gives advice.** It never changes a camera or the picture, and everything is measured on your computer. See [Checking your cameras match](https://github.com/braydenm32/cutaway/blob/v0.9.13/docs/guide/sources.md#checking-your-cameras-match).
- **Camera's own settings.** A capture card can't tell Cutaway what the camera itself is set to, so each camera now has a place to type its shutter, ISO, white balance and the rest. Fill it in once and the advice gets specific ("change white balance from 4300K to 4000K"). There's a list of things worth setting once on a camera, with one just for the Panasonic Lumix G85.
- **Warmth and Tint** join Brightness in **Picture adjustments**, for webcams and built-in cameras that have no settings of their own.
- **The pre-show check** has a **Camera check** line that says when the cameras were last checked.

**Several of the same camera**
- **Each camera shows its own picture.** Until now, if you had two or three cameras of the same make and model, every source showed the first one's picture. Cameras that share a name are now listed as "CamStream 4K", "CamStream 4K (2)", "CamStream 4K (3)", and each source remembers the exact camera you picked.
- **They're numbered by the USB port they're plugged into.** Keep each camera in the same port and it keeps its number. If you move one to another port, its source falls back to the camera with the same number, so check the pictures once before the show.
- **Existing shows:** a source you set up before this version still finds its camera by name. Pick the right camera for each one once in Sources, and from then on it remembers.

**Finer control in Edit layout**
- **Arrow keys move the picked frame** one pixel at a time (Shift + arrow: ten). With **✥ Move picture** on, or Option/Alt held, they move the picture inside the frame instead. A burst of presses is one step to undo.
- **Swap a source in one click.** Right-click a frame (or pick it and use **Frame › Swap for ▾**) and choose another source: it shows in the same place and size, keeping Fit or Fill, so *Cam 2 left* becomes *Cam 3 left*. A source that's already in the scene trades places with it, each picture keeping its own zoom.
- **TAKE and CUT move out of the way while you edit.** They now sit in the bar at the top beside **PREVIEW**, instead of over the picture's bottom-right corner, where they covered the resize handle of the right-hand frame.
